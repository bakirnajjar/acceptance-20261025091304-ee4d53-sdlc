## Proposed risks and verification

### RA4A9E-001 — Supplied sign-in appearance or control inventory drifts

**Category:** wireframe fidelity · **Priority:** should (planning: Medium) · **Requirements:** REQ-001, REQ-002, REQ-003, REQ-004, REQ-005, REQ-006, REQ-021 · **Evidence:** S1, S2.

- **Risk/consequence:** An observed label, placeholder, color/shape distinction or footer could be changed or omitted, or a visible sign-up or other control added. The card could fail wireframe acceptance and imply an unsupported journey.
- **Proposed mitigation:** Keep the white page, thin dark bordered card, bold Sign in heading, Email address and Password labels with `name@example.com` and masked-asterisks placeholders, blue underlined Forgot password? link, dark rectangular Sign in button, grey `No account? Contact your administrator.` footer, and no other visible card controls. Do not infer reset-page design from this image.
- **Proposed verification:** Inspect/render those elements and the absence of other visible card controls against the supplied observations; plan automated element/control assertions where feasible.

### RA4A9E-002 — Visible recovery link does not start a request

**Category:** recovery entry · **Priority:** must (planning: High) · **Requirements:** REQ-004, REQ-007, REQ-018 · **Evidence:** S1, S2, S3 (ACC-STD-01).

- **Risk/consequence:** The visually correct Forgot password? link could be inert or lead nowhere, preventing recovery.
- **Proposed mitigation:** Connect the observed link to a minimal reset-request interaction without adding another sign-in-card control.
- **Proposed verification:** Activate Forgot password? on the sign-in page in an automated interaction test and confirm a reset request can be initiated.

### RA4A9E-003 — Reset requests disclose account existence

**Category:** request privacy · **Priority:** must (planning: High) · **Requirements:** REQ-008, REQ-009, REQ-018 · **Evidence:** S3 (ACC-STD-01).

- **Risk/consequence:** Different returned responses for otherwise equivalent synthetic-account and nonexistent-address requests, including at the rate boundary, could disclose account existence. The standard does not settle nonexistent-address accounting.
- **Proposed mitigation:** Return identical request responses for otherwise equivalent existing/nonexistent submissions, including when an existing account's link issuance is limited. Do not invent a separate threshold for nonexistent addresses.
- **Proposed verification:** Compare returned responses for otherwise equivalent submissions and at the existing account's sixth request within an hour; confirm no extra usable link is issued and response equivalence remains. Response parity alone does not prove all side channels absent.

### RA4A9E-004 — Per-account issuance exceeds the hourly request limit

**Category:** request limits · **Priority:** must (planning: High) · **Requirements:** REQ-009, REQ-018 · **Evidence:** S3 (ACC-STD-01).

- **Risk/consequence:** Miscounting five requests per synthetic account per hour could issue a sixth usable link in the same hour.
- **Proposed mitigation:** Track synthetic-account requests against that limit in an explicitly testable one-hour window; do not assert a nonexistent-address key or threshold.
- **Proposed verification:** Exercise requests one through five and a sixth for one account in an hour; confirm the sixth creates no additional usable link, keeps REQ-008 response parity, and applies a one-hour rather than an arbitrary shorter window.

### RA4A9E-005 — Reset link reuse, expiry, or password change is mishandled

**Category:** link lifecycle · **Priority:** must (planning: High) · **Requirements:** REQ-010, REQ-011, REQ-012, REQ-018 · **Evidence:** S1, S3 (ACC-STD-02).

- **Risk/consequence:** A reused link might change a password again, a link might remain usable at/after T+30 minutes from issuance or expire prematurely, or a valid completion might not change its synthetic account password.
- **Proposed mitigation:** Associate links with their synthetic account and issuance time; permit at most one successful completion and expire them at 30 minutes **from issuance**, not from access. Do not invent a password-complexity rule.
- **Proposed verification:** Test otherwise valid unused links just before and at T+30 minutes; only the former remains unexpired. Complete a valid reset, confirm the corresponding account password changes, then retry the link and confirm another reset cannot complete.

### RA4A9E-006 — Successful reset leaves active sessions signed in

**Category:** session invalidation · **Priority:** must (planning: High) · **Requirements:** REQ-013, REQ-018 · **Evidence:** S3 (ACC-STD-02).

- **Risk/consequence:** A previously active session that survives a successful reset leaves a prior client signed in despite the all-session sign-out requirement.
- **Proposed mitigation:** Associate a minimal local synthetic-session representation with its account and invalidate every active session after successful reset; do not claim production session infrastructure.
- **Proposed verification:** Establish multiple active sessions for a synthetic account, complete a successful reset, and confirm none of those previously active sessions remains signed in.

### RA4A9E-007 — Owner notification is missing after a successful reset

**Category:** notification · **Priority:** must (planning: High) · **Requirements:** REQ-014, REQ-016, REQ-018 · **Evidence:** S1, S3 (ACC-STD-03).

- **Risk/consequence:** A successful reset might have no owner-directed notification, or only the first of multiple successes might be recorded; actual external delivery would exceed demonstration scope.
- **Proposed mitigation:** Record an owner-directed notification for **each** successful reset in a locally test-inspectable in-memory sink, without claiming email or SMS delivery.
- **Proposed verification:** Complete separate successful resets using distinct links and inspect the sink after each for its owner-directed record, without requiring a production service.

### RA4A9E-008 — Demonstration boundaries are hidden or overstated

**Category:** demonstration scope · **Priority:** should (planning: Medium) · **Requirements:** REQ-015, REQ-016, REQ-017, REQ-018 · **Evidence:** S1.

- **Risk/consequence:** Real-user accounts, external notification dependencies, or missing production-limitations documentation could violate scope or suggest production readiness.
- **Proposed mitigation:** Use only synthetic accounts and in-memory notifications; document both and any further implementation-specific limitations once known, without implying persistence, actual delivery, or production guarantees.
- **Proposed verification:** Inspect account fixtures and notification mechanism; check that the production-limitations section names both constraints and avoids a production-readiness claim; plan automated synthetic/in-memory checks.

### RA4A9E-009 — Automated tests omit approved acceptance boundaries

**Category:** acceptance coverage · **Priority:** must (planning: High) · **Requirements:** REQ-001, REQ-002, REQ-003, REQ-004, REQ-005, REQ-006, REQ-007, REQ-008, REQ-009, REQ-010, REQ-011, REQ-012, REQ-013, REQ-014, REQ-015, REQ-016, REQ-018, REQ-021 · **Evidence:** S1, S2, S3.

- **Risk/consequence:** A happy-path-only suite could miss card/control drift, inert recovery entry, response disclosure, hourly limit, link reuse/expiry, password change, session invalidation, notification after every success, or synthetic/in-memory boundaries, leaving acceptance unverified.
- **Proposed mitigation:** Plan automated tests against REQ-018 and the linked approved IDs with negative and time-boundary cases; base visual assertions on supplied observations, not imagined pixel specifications.
- **Proposed verification:** Review and run the planned suite for card elements/no extra controls, recovery entry, existing/nonexistent response parity, five-per-account-per-hour boundary, single-use, expiry at T+30 minutes from issuance, correct password change, all-session sign-out, every-success notification, and synthetic/in-memory behavior. No test execution is claimed here.

### RA4A9E-010 — Delivery breaches the no-deployment boundary

**Category:** delivery boundary · **Priority:** must (planning: High) · **Requirements:** REQ-019, REQ-020 · **Evidence:** S1.

- **Risk/consequence:** Deployment of the application or addition of a deployment workflow violates two **separate** approved prohibitions, regardless of local functionality.
- **Proposed mitigation:** Keep delivery local; exclude deployment actions and deployment-workflow additions.
- **Proposed verification:** Review delivered actions/artifacts for absence of deployment (REQ-019), and separately inspect workflow changes for absence of added deployment workflows (REQ-020). Do not deploy as verification.

## Bounded assumptions

- The entries are proposed delivery risks and future verification, not observed defects, tests run, measured likelihood, or implemented architecture.
- The supplied image specifies only the observed sign-in card; it does not specify reset screens, transport, persistence, password complexity, or production infrastructure.
- Only minimal local synthetic sign-in/session behavior is assumed to exercise the page and all-session invalidation; no production session system is implied.
- Enforce five requests per synthetic account per hour with existing/nonexistent response parity; no separate nonexistent-address threshold or counting rule is asserted.
- A test-inspectable local mechanism can expose synthetic reset links and in-memory notifications, without real email, SMS, or external delivery.
- Minimal reset completion changes the corresponding synthetic account password; no unsupported complexity rule, numeric policy, failure wording, or extra sign-in-card control is assumed.
- Production-limitations documentation names at least the synthetic-account and in-memory-notification constraints and any further implementation-specific constraints once known, without a production-readiness claim.
- The approved predecessor supplied REQ-001–REQ-021 but no source-reference object; original evidence is cited rather than an invented predecessor locator.

## Non-blocking open questions

1. **Sign-in and sessions:** What minimal Sign in behavior and synthetic-account session representation will demonstrate invalidation of every active session? Only local demonstration behavior is assumed.
2. **Request accounting:** How will nonexistent-address attempts be counted or throttled without revealing existence, and what defines an account key? Keep response parity and the specified synthetic-account limit; invent no separate threshold.
3. **Local inspection:** How will reset links be accessible and the in-memory sink inspected in tests? Assume only a local, test-inspectable mechanism, not external delivery.
4. **Further limits:** Which additional production limitations emerge from eventual implementation? At minimum document synthetic accounts and in-memory notifications; make no unsupported production-readiness assertion.
5. **Reset completion:** What minimal form, validation, and failure wording will be used? The sign-in image does not establish these details or a password-complexity rule.
